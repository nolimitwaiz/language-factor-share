## Experiment A — §3.1.2 corrected lens probe
Setup: unembed per-layer last-token states (final-norm then output embedding),
non-Latin-script languages only (rus, zho, jpn, hin, ben, tam, tel, amh, khm,
kat, mya) so input-language vs Latin/English token mass is separable by
Unicode script. Models: Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B + OLMo-2-1B. N=100 NTREX sentences.
Also measured: mean max-cosine of hidden states to output-embedding rows per
layer ("lens validity" — the proposal's concern (1) quantified).

- A1: Every model shows a mid-layer valley in input-script token mass
  (the lens-visible funnel).
- A2: The valley's LOCATION coincides with the LFS dip: the argmin of
  input-script mass falls within ±15% relative depth of the LFS minimum.
- A3: Latin/English token mass in the valley DECREASES with Qwen3 scale
  (mirror of eng-best-donor 45→25/127): 0.6B > 4B on mean mid-layer
  Latin-mass over non-Latin inputs.
- A4 (proposal concern 1, quantified): lens validity (max cosine to output
  embeddings) is materially lower in middle layers than final layers —
  i.e., raw Logit Lens conclusions about middle layers stand on weak ground,
  as the proposal suspected.

## Experiment B — §3.1.3 cross-lingual context benefit (the pitcher/bat design)
Setup: NTREX adjacent same-document sentence pairs (s1, s2), target s2 ALWAYS
English (holds target fluency constant); context s1 in {eng, deu, fra, spa,
rus, zho, hin, tur, vie, ben, swa, amh, khm}. Context benefit
Δ_L = mean NLL(s2|∅) − mean NLL(s2|s1_L); ratio R_L = Δ_L / Δ_eng.
Models: Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B + OLMo-2-1B. ~100 pairs.

- B1: R_L > 0 for all high-resource contexts in Qwen3 models (cross-lingual
  context is USED, not ignored).
- B2: R_L correlates positively with the same model's per-language alignment
  (MEXA at best layer) across the 12 non-English context languages —
  Spearman > 0.5 for Qwen3-4B. THIS IS THE CENTRAL TEST: intrinsic alignment
  should predict cross-lingual information USE in generation.
- B3: mean R_L (non-English) increases with Qwen3 scale (0.6B < 1.7B < 4B).
- B4: OLMo-2-1B shows near-zero R_L for non-European-script contexts
  (mix-beats-scale echo: an EN-centric recipe can't use what it never aligned).

Analysis code frozen with this file: code/lens_312.py, code/transfer_313.py.

## AIM-2 FEASIBILITY PILOT — pre-registered 2026-07-07, before any training run
Question: is LFS usable as a training objective (the Aim-2 handoff claim)?
Setup: fine-tune Qwen3-0.6B-Base for ~400 steps with loss = LM_loss +
lambda * batch-LFS (differentiable variance share at the best layer, computed
on parallel batches), training text = NTREX sentence indices 500-1900
(DISJOINT from the 1-300 evaluation sentences), ~16 languages + English.
Predictions:
- P-A2.1 (sanity): the trained model's LFS dip deepens vs base (dip 0.206
  at base; predict >= +0.03 deeper).
- P-A2.2 (transfer beyond the objective): mean MEXA at best layer improves
  for mid+low-tier languages (any positive delta).
- P-A2.3 (no collapse — the critical one): 0-shot Belebele aggregate does not
  drop by more than 0.01 vs base (0.368). If it collapses, LFS-as-objective
  is falsified in this naive form and reported as such.
- P-A2.4 (stretch): Belebele improves on low-tier languages.
Confound note: training on NTREX-domain text may improve alignment metrics
via domain adaptation alone; a control (same training WITHOUT the LFS term,
lambda=0, same steps/data) is run alongside — predictions compare
LFS-trained vs lambda-0 control, not vs base alone.
