\begin{center}
{\LARGE\textbf{What the study found}}\\[3pt]
{\small LFS, RMFS, and a model that lost its meaning structure ---
Waiz Khan, JHU, August 2026}
\end{center}
\vspace{-2pt}\hrule\vspace{8pt}

\textbf{A degraded model scored best.} Across 25 training variants of
Qwen3-0.6B (75 checkpoints), the variant trained with the word level
alignment objective lost about \textbf{95\%} of the structure in its
representations that encodes meaning rather than language. It posted
the \textbf{best LFS score in the study}. MEXA ranked it third of the
25.

\textbf{The ratio cannot see this.} LFS is a share: meaning structure
against language structure. When a model shrinks both together the
share does not move. Synthetic tests give the closed form --- the share
shifts by less than a millionth while meaning structure falls by 99
percent. Any scale invariant ratio inherits this. The possibility was
written into the July preregistration, together with the detector that
catches it, before any of these models were trained.

\textbf{The damage cost it nothing.} On a 40 language reading test the
degraded variant scores \textbf{0.395} against \textbf{0.390} for the
untouched control. The variants that did lose ability, by roughly six
points, look clean on every geometric measure taken. Representational
damage and capability damage are separable, and the measures that see
one do not see the other.

\textbf{RMFS reports four readings per language.} Each carries its own
validated number, and each predicts something the others miss:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
reading & what it tracks & correlation\\
\midrule
Meaning against language & carrying content across languages &
$\mathbf{0.76}$\\
Sentence matching & downstream exam performance & $0.24$--$0.31$\\
Weakest aligned languages & which variants lost ability &
$\mathbf{0.51}$\\
Collapse flag & the case above & exact\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The first is LFS's original measurement, kept intact. Correlations are
measured after removing the effects of training data volume, language
family, script and tokenizer differences.

\textbf{Scores read as percentiles.} For a model: embed 300 parallel
sentences in 41 languages at one layer, compute the four readings per
language, convert each to a percentile against a reference grid of 14
models, average. A score of 74 means better than 74 percent of the
reference models. Roughly four GPU minutes per model. The ordering
falls out with no tuning: Qwen3-8B \textbf{90.5}, 4B \textbf{85.7},
1.7B \textbf{73.8}; the multilingual focused Salamandra-7B
\textbf{69.0}; the English centric OLMo-2-1B \textbf{28.6}.

\textbf{What the mapping between languages looks like.} Across 1{,}792
model and language pairs the fitted map is linear or simpler in
\textbf{74 percent} of cases. The nonlinear cases are concentrated
rather than spread out: three models carry 80 percent of them, and
across the other eleven only 6.5 percent of languages need a nonlinear
map.
