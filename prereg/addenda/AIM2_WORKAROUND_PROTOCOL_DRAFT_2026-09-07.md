# Aim 2 Workaround Protocol, DRAFT for review

Status: draft for Waiz Khan and Philipp Koehn to amend and freeze. Nothing here is preregistered yet. Dated preregistration addenda (one per experiment) are written and frozen before any launch, and each experiment needs a GPU-hour approval per project rules. Synthesized from three panel programs and two judges' scores on 2026-09-07; the appendix lists what was verified against repository artifacts while drafting and two design errors that verification caught.

## 1. Premise

Aim 1 built a measurement, not a training target. LFS is the share of variance in mean-pooled hidden states that lies between languages rather than between sentences, computed per layer on a 128-language by 300-sentence grid of matched NTREX translations after joint per-coordinate z-scoring, with a pure-noise value of (L-1)/(L+N-2) = 0.298 at that grid shape. Every model measured (17 of 17 primary, 19 of 19 expansion, 17 of 17 on FLORES-200) shows an interior minimum with recovery at both ends; the depth of that minimum ranges from 0.039 (BLOOM-1.7B) to 0.336 (Qwen3-8B), tracks training recipe more than parameter count, moves by at most 0.008 under instruction tuning, and at the dip layer 1-LFS predicts cross-lingual content transfer (Spearman 0.508, model-bootstrap CI [0.19, 0.71], 19 models, 13 families) while predicting nothing about pooled exam accuracy after confound removal. The misalignment budget says most of what separates languages at the dip is an additive per-language offset plus an isotropic per-language scale (65 to 98 percent of held-out misalignment; rotation under 1 percent). The Aim 2 campaign then showed why the metric cannot be the loss: minimizing LFS reversed the observational association (LFS and tail alignment moved together at Pearson +0.924 across 18 arm-seed points); the proposal's word-alignment squared-difference loss collapsed representation norm from 451 to 42 and raw concept variance from 13,791 to 699 while LFS, MEXA, and AaR all read as fine or better, and only the raw concept-variance preservation reading (CVP) caught it; a bounded CVP hinge could not restrain that unbounded term at any weight; word- and phrase-level code-switched training gave no detectable benefit and a monotone cost on unseen languages, with a 25 percent replay mixture the one clear success. The PI has said Aim 2 need not depend on the metric-as-loss idea. This protocol therefore uses the Aim 1 readings in exactly three roles, layer selector, collapse monitor with pre-declared stop rules, and manipulation check, and never as a loss. The interventions are standard operations an NLP, MT, or ML reader already knows: per-language mean removal at inference time (the operation behind language-neutral sentence retrieval, libovicky2020neutrality), continued language-model training on differently composed data at matched token budgets, and a scale-invariant word-alignment term trained with rehearsal. Success is judged on behavior the audience already trusts: cross-lingual content transfer on the frozen harness, held-out perplexity, and exam accuracy as a non-inferiority check. MEXA is computed alongside as a published reference measure.

## 2. The program

Ordered by value per GPU-hour. W0 costs no GPU time and gates W1. W1's pilot is the under-one-GPU-hour experiment in Section 3. W2 and W3 close proposal sub-aims 3.2.1 and 3.2.2 at the 0.6B scale on existing code. W4 is a conditional tier that is specified but not costed as firm.

Common rules for every experiment: the intervention layer is the argmin of the per-layer LFS curve in results/grid/<model>/metrics.json (or results/instruct_grid for post-trained models), never re-searched; every LFS number is printed with its pure-noise value and its SS_lang, SS_con, SS_res components; CVP against the frozen model, mean representation norm, and effective rank are logged wherever a model is trained or hooked; MEXA and AaR@{1,5,10,20} are computed alongside as reference and tail readings; contrasts in trained arms are against the matched control arm, never against the frozen model; a reading that moves favorably while CVP or norm leaves its band is scored as a failure whatever LFS or MEXA say; every run writes a manifest (code hash, input hashes, seed, precision, host, wall time); nothing in results/dumps or results/grid is ever overwritten.

### W0. Offline pre-test on the saved 14-model dumps (0 GPU-hours)

Question. When the additive per-language offset and the per-language isotropic scale at the dip layer are removed exactly and in closed form, with no model loaded, how does each reading family respond (variance partition: LFS with components; order-based retrieval: MEXA, AaR tail curve; scale: raw concept variance, norm, effective rank)? How much language identity survives centering and scaling for a linear and a nonlinear probe? And how much of the per-language SS_lang share is pretraining exposure and tokenizer fertility?

Hypothesis. Offset removal removes most of the language variance the additive decomposition measures and leaves concept variance untouched by construction. Retrieval readings respond to centering (prior from the centering literature and from the project's own whitened check, where Qwen3-0.6B-Base mean MEXA at the dip moved from 0.294 to 0.483 and LFS from 0.709 to 0.369), but the direction and size are recorded as an empirical outcome, not derived; the earlier claim that a per-language shift is invisible to cosine retrieval was wrong and is withdrawn. Per-language isotropic rescaling is exactly invisible to cosine retrieval readings but visible to the variance partition and to any downstream layer, which is why W1 carries a scale condition. Language identity is almost entirely additive-plus-scale, so probes on centered-and-scaled states fall near chance; a model where they do not carries non-additive language information the decomposition does not see.

Frozen predictions.
- P1 (manipulation check). Under I1 (subtract b_l = per-language mean minus grand mean, fitted on calibration sentences 300 to 1499 and applied to evaluation sentences 0 to 299), dip-layer LFS falls by at least 0.10 and SS_lang by at least 50 percent in 14 of 14 models; raw concept variance ratio equals 1.000 to three decimals (exact by construction; a deviation is a bug).
- P2 (retrieval, empirical, both outcomes pre-labeled). Mean MEXA over the 127 non-English languages under I1 changes by at least 0.010 in at least 10 of 14 models: retrieval readings "respond to the additive component" and remain admissible as secondary readings for offset interventions. Absolute change below 0.010 in at least 10 of 14: retrieval readings are "near-invariant" and W1 is evaluated on behavior only. Anything else is reported per model as mixed. Direction is reported; the prior is an increase.
- P3 (scale, exact and moderated). I2 (subtract b_l, then rescale each language's centered cloud to the consensus RMS) leaves MEXA and AaR@10 identical to I1 to three decimals in 14 of 14 (a code check, since cosine is scale-invariant within a language) and lowers the residual share SS_res relative to I1; across the 13 models with a budget file, the size of that residual drop rank-correlates with the budget's median M2 (scale) share at Spearman at least 0.5 (Qwen3-0.6B-Base median M1 0.329 versus M2 0.367; Qwen3-1.7B-Base 0.350 versus 0.383; OLMo-2-1124-7B 0.778 versus 0.190, so OLMo-2-7B should show the smallest residual drop among the Qwen3 and OLMo models).
- P4 (control). I3 (subtract a random unit direction scaled to the norm of b_l, per language, three seeds) changes mean MEXA by less than half of the I1 change in 12 of 14 models; if I3 matches I1 within 50 percent, the I1 effect is a magnitude artifact. The I3 effect itself is reported, not predicted.
- P5 (calibration size). Offsets fitted on 300 calibration sentences reproduce the 1,200-sentence LFS drop within 0.02 in 14 of 14 models. Failure means the offsets are not stable estimands at the sizes W1 uses and W1 does not launch until they are refit on more sentences.
- P6 (probes, document-purged splits). On the 24-language subset of the existing premise test, linear probe accuracy on raw z-scored dip states is at least 0.80 in 14 of 14 (replicating 0.875 to 0.886 on Qwen3-0.6B; chance 0.042). After per-language centering fitted on the training documents, linear accuracy is below 0.10 and a 256-unit MLP below 0.20 in at least 12 of 14; after centering plus per-language RMS scaling, MLP below 0.15. Any model whose centered-and-scaled MLP accuracy stays above 0.30 is flagged as carrying non-additive language information, and the budget's rotation figure is contradicted for that model. A length-and-token-count-only probe supplies the floor; all probe numbers are reported net of it.
- P7 (share anatomy, descriptive with a gate). Within each of the 14 models, Spearman between per-language SS_lang share at the dip (per_lang_share_dip_full1500) and log exposure lies between -0.30 and -0.75 over the roughly 100 languages with a CulturaX or CC-100 count; fertility Spearman positive in all 14. If |rho| with exposure exceeds 0.85 in 10 or more models, the share is treated as an exposure reading and W4c is not proposed. The verified top-share languages in Qwen3-0.6B-Base (div 4.4x uniform, bod 3.0x, dzo 3.0x, mri 2.7x, sin 2.2x, mya 2.2x, ton 2.1x, khm 1.8x, lao 1.8x, tir 1.8x) make the exposure explanation the leading one.

Models and data. All 14 dump models (Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B-Base, OLMo-2-0425-1B, OLMo-2-1124-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, bloom-1b7, bloom-7b1, EuroLLM-1.7B, SmolLM2-1.7B, Falcon3-7B-Base, salamandra-2b, salamandra-7b). Inputs: results/dumps/<model>/layerNNN.npz on CLSP (128 languages x 1,500 sentences, fp32 pooled then fp16 stored, five layers per model: 0, first quartile, dip, third quartile, last; for Qwen3-1.7B-Base the set is 0, 7, 13, 21, 28), margins files for tail curves, results/dip_bootstrap/<model>/bootstrap.json, results/budget/<model>/budget.json, results/whitened/<model>.json, data/NTREX/DOCUMENT_IDS.tsv, the exposure and fertility tables used by code/attribution.py and code/fertility.py. The fp16 storage quantization check in dump_embeddings.py is rerun first.

Method. Per model and dumped layer: fit b_l, the grand mean m, and s_l on the calibration split; apply I1 (x - b_l), I2 (m + (x - mu_l)/s_l), I3 (x - r_l), and I5 (x - b_l + b_eng, English swap) to the evaluation split; recompute LFS with components after joint z-scoring exactly as in code/pilot_metrics.py, MEXA and AaR@{1,5,10,20} against the English pivot, raw concept variance ratio, mean norm, per-language RMS, effective rank; sentence bootstrap with 2,000 replicates following src/analysis/dip_bootstrap.py. Probes: multinomial logistic and one-hidden-layer MLP on raw, centered, and centered-and-scaled states, split roughly 70/30 by NTREX document id, per-language log-loss recorded and correlated with per_lang_share_dip_full1500. Share anatomy: regress share on log exposure, fertility, script, and macro-family with language-cluster bootstrap. Primary contrast: I1 versus identity at the dip; everything else Holm-corrected within model.

Controls. Random direction of matched norm (I3), off-dip layers (four other dumped layers), English swap (I5), length-only probe floor.

Monitoring readings. LFS profile at the five dumped layers with components and pure-noise value; raw concept variance ratio; norm; effective rank; MEXA; AaR curve and the count of mean-positive but tail-negative languages.

Behavioral targets. None; this is the gate for W1 and produces the offset tables W1 consumes.

Cost. 0 GPU-hours. About 4 CPU-hours for the offset conditions (largest D=4096, 128 x 300 x 5 layers x 5 conditions) plus about 30 CPU-hours for probes as a cpu-partition array job (14 models x 5 layers).

Code to write. src/aim2/offset_offline.py (about 200 lines: purged split, offset and scale fit, four interventions, readings, bootstrap, cross-model table, offset tables written to results/aim2/offsets/<model>/layer<dip>_offsets.npz with sha256). A dump-reading mode for src/aim2/adversarial_probe.py with document-purged splits, the three conditions, per-language log-loss, and the floor probe (about 150 lines). src/analysis/share_anatomy.py (about 120 lines) joining bootstrap shares, exposure, fertility, script, family. tests/test_offset_offline.py asserting raw concept variance ratio 1.000 under I1 and I3, exact invertibility of I1, and MEXA invariance under I2 relative to I1. Reused: src/analysis/budget_real.py load_layer, src/geometry/ladder.py _fit_offset and _fit_scale, src/battery/inject.py i1_offset, code/pilot_metrics.py lfs, mexa_score, aar, src/analysis/dip_bootstrap.py lfs_components.

Risks. Calibration and evaluation share the NTREX domain (a FLORES-200 calibration check is named as follow-up). Offsets estimated on pooled states speak to pooled geometry; W1 tests tokens. A 128-class logistic fit on about 134k rows by up to 4,096 dimensions can take an hour per fit on CPU; iteration caps are recorded. Near-identical variants (four English variants, Bosnian-Croatian-Serbian) cap 128-class probe accuracy, so the 24-language subset is primary and the 128-class run merges variants into one class. No optimization, so no Goodhart pressure; the selection risk is in choosing W1 models after seeing W0, which is why the W1 model set is frozen in this document.

### W1. Training-free removal of the dip-layer language offset as a forward hook, scored on behavior (pilot 1 GPU-hour; extension about 16, conditional)

Question. Does subtracting the additive per-language offset (and, in a second condition, also normalizing the per-language scale) from the residual stream at the dip layer, at inference time with no training, change what the model does: its use of foreign-language context when predicting English text (content transfer), its same-language next-token prediction (perplexity), and its reading-comprehension accuracy (Belebele)? Is the dip layer, chosen from the LFS profile, the layer at which this trade is best?

Hypothesis. Two programs committed opposite frozen predictions, so this experiment cannot come back uninformative. Reading A (Constrain and Monitor): the dip-layer offset is a language-identity signal that later layers use to keep predicting in the input language and is separable from content; removing it from foreign-language context makes that content more usable for an English target, so content transfer rises, same-language perplexity rises modestly, and Belebele is unchanged. Reading B (Re-specified): the offset is behaviorally inert at this layer because later layers re-derive language identity from tokens, so nothing moves; that would be consistent with the campaign's finding that reducing language variance did not improve behavior. Reading C (pre-labeled construct failure): content transfer falls while perplexity rises, meaning the offset carries content and the additive language-versus-concept split is contradicted for behavior.

Frozen predictions (extension; the pilot's own decision rule is in Section 3).
- P1 (manipulation check on the hooked model). Pooled dip-layer states of the hooked model on 300 evaluation sentences x 128 languages reproduce W0's I1 readings: LFS down at least 0.10 and SS_lang down at least 50 percent in 8 of 8 models; raw concept variance ratio within [0.98, 1.02] (the first-token guard makes the hooked pooled mean differ from the exact offline centering by one token in n). Exploratory: last-layer LFS stays within 0.05 of the unintervened value.
- P2 (primary behavioral). Median R_content over the 40 non-English context languages under I1 minus identity is at least +0.05, with the 100-pair paired bootstrap 95 percent interval excluding zero, in at least 5 of 8 models. R_content is computed with the identity condition's English content as the fixed denominator; raw content in nats is reported alongside.
- P3 (same-language cost). Held-out per-language perplexity on NTREX sentences 0 to 299 across 128 languages rises under I1 by a geometric-mean factor between 1.00 and 1.30 in at least 6 of 8; a factor above 1.30 marks the intervention as damaging for that model regardless of P2.
- P4 (capability non-inferiority, small models only). Belebele 0-shot pooled accuracy over 122 languages under I1 is within 1.0 point of identity and worst-decile languages within 2.0 points for Qwen3-1.7B-Base, OLMo-2-0425-1B, and EuroLLM-1.7B (pooled standard error about 0.25 points).
- P5 (control). I3 changes median R_content by less than 0.02 in absolute value with the perplexity factor within [0.99, 1.02] in 8 of 8; if I3 matches I1, the effect is a perturbation artifact.
- P6 (scale co-primary). I2 does not fall below I1 on median R_content by more than 0.02 in at least 6 of 8. Exploratory direction: I2 exceeds I1 in the Qwen3 models, where the budget's scale rung carries as much held-out misalignment as the offset.
- P7 (per-language targeting). Within each model, the per-language content gain under I1 rank-correlates with the per-language SS_lang share at the dip at Spearman at least 0.30 in at least 5 of 8.
- P8 (layer selection, two models). On Qwen3-1.7B-Base and OLMo-2-1124-7B, the ratio of content-transfer gain to perplexity cost is largest at the dip among the first-quartile, dip, and third-quartile dumped layers.
- P9 (English hub). I5 does not exceed I1 on median R_content by more than 0.02 in at least 6 of 8.
- P10 (moderator, reported not scored). Mistral-7B-v0.3 (isotropic language structure in the whitened check; dip at layer 2 of 33) is in the bottom two on P2.

Models. Frozen now, independent of W0 results: Qwen3-1.7B-Base (pilot; dip 13), Qwen3-8B-Base (dip 19; deepest dip, 0.336), OLMo-2-0425-1B (dip 9), OLMo-2-1124-7B (dip 12; offset-dominated budget), Mistral-7B-v0.3 (dip 2), bloom-7b1 (dip 17; bf16 or fp32, never fp16), EuroLLM-1.7B (dip 14), salamandra-7b (dip 18). Qwen3-0.6B-Base (dip 8) is the smoke-test model and is not scored.

Data. Offsets from W0's tables (calibration sentences 300 to 1499 with the sentence-level purge described in Section 3; a whole-document purge is infeasible, see Appendix). Content transfer: the frozen R1 protocol (100 pairs from 100 distinct documents, 41 context languages including English, matched and mismatched contexts, teacher-forced English target NLL, 2,048-token ceiling, bf16; 8,300 sequences per model per condition). Perplexity: NTREX sentences 0 to 299, 128 languages. Belebele: the existing 0-shot protocol behind results/belebele0. Representation readings: 300 evaluation sentences x 128 languages at the five dumped layers.

Method. A forward hook on the decoder block whose output is hidden_states[dip] subtracts, at every token position except the first (BOS and massive-activation guard), the offset of that position's language given a token-level language mask; I2 applies m + (h - mu_l)/s_l instead. Conditions: I0 identity; I1; I2; I3 random direction; I5 English swap (P9); I4, the I1 hook at the first-quartile and third-quartile layers on two models (P8). Context tokens carry the context language (English context tokens are hooked with b_eng under the same rule); target tokens are unhooked; no-context sequences are unhooked, which makes their NLL a determinism check across conditions. Magnitude fixed at 1.0 times the estimated offset; a 0.5 and 1.5 sweep on Qwen3-1.7B-Base is labeled exploratory and excluded from claims. Belebele runs for I0, I1, I3 on the three small models only. Joint decision rule: P2 and P3 and P5 together; P4 as non-inferiority; the rest Holm-corrected.

Controls. Random direction of matched norm; off-dip layers; English swap; unhooked no-context NLL; nearest-centroid language identification in place of gold labels as a deployability secondary on two models.

Monitoring readings. LFS with components at the five dumped layers on the hooked model; raw concept variance ratio (must stay within 2 percent); mean norm and per-language RMS; effective rank; MEXA and AaR@{1,5,10,20}; the hook's mean subtracted norm as a fraction of residual norm by position.

Behavioral targets. R_content and raw content per context language (primary); same-language perplexity factor (cost); Belebele pooled and worst-decile (non-inferiority).

Cost. Pilot 1 GPU-hour (Section 3). Extension: content transfer plus perplexity plus five-layer hooked extraction, four conditions, about 0.3 GPU-h per condition for 1 to 2B models and 0.55 for 7 to 9B (2 x 4 x 0.3 + 5 x 4 x 0.55 = 13.4); I5 on all seven at about 0.45 average (3.2); Belebele on three small models, three conditions (2.7); layer controls on two models (1.5); total about 21 before contingency; ask 16 firm-if-gated plus 5 contingency. Gate: the pilot returns Reading A (extension on all seven), Reading C (extension on three models to confirm the construct failure), or not-detectable (extension on the three small models only). Reading B stops W1 at the pilot.

Code to write. src/aim2/hooks.py (about 150 lines: block resolution for Qwen3, OLMo-2, Mistral, BLOOM, EuroLLM, Salamandra; token-language mask; first-token guard; offset-table loader; I1, I2, I3, I5 modes). A wrapper around the R1 harness functions (read_pairs, load_texts, build_specs, encode_spec, score_batches, summarize from src/analysis/r1_content_transfer.py) that installs the hook and writes the paired bootstrap (about 200 lines). A hooked extraction reusing evaluate.py embed and layer_stats (about 60 lines). An lm-evaluation-harness HFLM subclass that installs the hook and passes the task language (about 80 lines, extension only). tests/test_aim2_hooks.py on CPU with a tiny model: hooked block output equals output_hidden_states[dip]; hooked pooled states equal offline-centered pooled states within fp32 tolerance when the guard is disabled; raw concept variance unchanged; I1 followed by adding b_l back is the identity; no-context sequences bit-identical to unhooked.

Risks. Pooled offsets applied per token are an approximation; the per-position diagnostic is logged and a token-level offset variant estimated on 200 fresh sentences per language is run on one model as sensitivity. Gold language labels for the mask; the nearest-centroid secondary covers deployment. Six architectures' block naming; the CPU test and the 0.6B smoke run come first. BLOOM precision rule. Domain is shared between calibration and evaluation (both NTREX), so a FLORES-200-calibrated replication is named as follow-up. Later layers may re-derive language identity, which is Reading B and is informative.

### W2. Closing proposal 3.2.1: parallel-text reference arm and sentence-level alternation on the existing code-switch protocol, plus forgetting versus share on existing checkpoints (about 11 GPU-hours)

Question. Measured against the same monolingual control (CS-B), the same 25 percent replay mixture, step budget, and preregistered criteria as the code-switch arms, does training on English-source parallel text (the comparison the proposal treats as established) or on sentence-by-sentence alternating text within a document (the third granularity the proposal names and the one not yet built) move any reading or behavior? Add-on: across all 128 NTREX languages, does the frozen model's per-language SS_lang share at the dip predict how much each uncovered language degraded under the nine existing LM-only control checkpoints, after exposure and fertility controls?

Hypothesis. The code-switch null currently has no parallel-text comparison, so it cannot say whether code-switching underperforms parallel text or whether data composition of any kind is inert at this budget. The proposal's premise predicts parallel text improves mean-case retrieval on trained languages; sentence-level alternation behaves like parallel text rather than like word-level switching; neither reaches the unseen-language cost of word-level switching. Whatever the outcome, 3.2.1 is closed at this scale after W2, and code-switching is not carried into any scale-up unless sentence-level alternation passes where parallel text fails. For the add-on, the share identifies fragile languages beyond resource tier; the alternative (it is exposure under another name) removes the share as an allocation key.

Frozen predictions.
- P1. CS-PAR improves trained-language MEXA over CS-B by at least 0.018 (the preregistered minimum detectable effect) in 3 of 3 seeds at 400 steps; below that, parallel text is also inert at this budget and the code-switch null is not a deficiency specific to code-switching.
- P2. Tail AaR@10 does not fall below CS-B by more than 0.010 in either arm.
- P3. CVP on the final checkpoint at least 0.90 and dip-layer norm within 10 percent of CS-B in both arms (no alignment term, so collapse should be unreachable; recall that CS-W50 failed a CVP checkpoint criterion with data alone, so this is a live gate, not a formality).
- P4. Unseen-language (outside replay) geometric-mean perplexity ratio: CS-S within CS-B plus one percentage point; CS-PAR at most the ratio measured for CS-W25 at the same step count.
- P5. Belebele 41-task accuracy within 1.0 point of CS-B for both arms.
- P6. Context use at 200 pairs, minimum detectable difference 0.052 nats per language as preregistered; below that, not detectable.
- P7 (domain check). Trained-language MEXA on the FLORES-mapped 300-sentence set moves in the same direction as on NTREX for any arm passing P1; an NTREX-only gain is recorded as domain memorization, not transfer.
- A1 (add-on). For each of the 9 existing LM-only checkpoints (arms B, CS-B, WA-B, seeds 0 to 2, listed in manifests/aim2_scientific_checkpoints_2026-08-24.txt), Spearman between the frozen model's per-language share and the per-language log perplexity ratio over the genuinely uncovered languages is at least 0.40; A2: after partialling out log exposure and fertility, the partial association stays positive with a language-cluster bootstrap interval excluding zero in at least 6 of 9. Failure of A2 removes the share as an allocation key (W4c is not proposed).

Models and data. Qwen3-0.6B-Base (dip layer 8), matching the code-switch campaign whose CS-B, CS-W25, and CS-P25 checkpoints and evaluations (400 and 2,000 steps) are reused without re-running. NTREX sentences 500 to 1900 in the 14 code-switch languages with DOCUMENT_IDS.tsv for document-coherent alternation; the existing replay set; evaluation on NTREX first 300 sentences (12-language split), FLORES-mapped 300 sentences via data/FLORES200_ntrex_to_flores.json, 200 context-use pairs, Belebele 41 tasks, per-language perplexity including yor and zul. Add-on: NTREX first 300 sentences for all 128 languages on the nine checkpoints plus the frozen model.

Method. Two new corpus fields built by src/aim2/codeswitch.py: CS-PAR (English source sentence followed by its translation into one of the 14 languages, both orders, separator recorded) and CS-S (sentence i in English, sentence i+1 in language l, alternating within a document). Tokens per step held equal to CS-B by fixing the token budget per batch rather than the sentence count. 400 steps x 3 seeds for both arms; 2,000 steps x 2 seeds to match the existing 2,000-step code-switch arms. Same optimizer, replay fraction, and evaluation stack as the campaign; contrasts against CS-B only; scored by scorecard_codeswitch.py rules with a dated addendum to prereg/PREREG_CODESWITCH.md adding P7. Add-on: extend evaluate.py's perplexity pass to all 128 languages, run on the nine checkpoints (about 0.1 GPU-h each), join with share, exposure, fertility, script, family.

Controls. CS-B as the control arm; the existing CS-W25 and CS-P25 as the code-switch comparison set; FLORES-side evaluation; exposure groups derived from each study's training manifest at scoring time.

Monitoring readings. Every 20 steps on the probe grid: batch LFS with pure-noise value, CVP against frozen, effective rank, LM loss, tripwire flag. Per checkpoint: LFS profile over all 29 layers with components; raw concept variance and CVP; norm; MEXA and AaR@{1,5,10,20} on trained and held-out groups, NTREX and FLORES; per-language perplexity with replay-covered and uncovered languages separated.

Behavioral targets. Cross-lingual context gain at 200 pairs; Belebele 41-task accuracy; unseen-language perplexity.

Cost. Training 6 x 0.12 + 4 x 0.6 = about 3.1 GPU-h; evaluation 10 checkpoints x about 0.65 = 6.5; add-on about 1.0; total about 11.

Code to write. CS-PAR and CS-S corpus builders with document-aware alternation and token-budget matching (about 120 lines), two ARMS entries in train_codeswitch.py, a FLORES-side evaluation group in evaluate.py (about 30 lines), a 128-language perplexity mode and src/aim2/forgetting_vs_share.py (about 120 lines), the prereg addendum.

Risks. A MEXA gain can come from memorizing NTREX-domain sentence pairs (P7 guards). Parallel examples are twice as long, so token matching means fewer parallel examples per step; stated as a design choice. NTREX target sides are English-sourced translations. Sentence alternation needs document boundaries; about 90 documents in the training range, so CS-S is smaller and reported with its size. Add-on depends on the nine checkpoints having been retained; if not, about 1 GPU-h of retraining per control arm. One model, one budget.

### W3. Proposal 3.2.2 re-specified: cosine word-level alignment with the language-identity vector, replay, and a weight sweep (stage 1 about 9 GPU-hours; stage 2 about 15, conditional)

Question. Does the proposal's word-level alignment term, written as cosine distance so that it cannot be satisfied by shrinking representations, improve retrieval tail alignment or cross-lingual context use over a matched LM-only control at any weight, once held-out forgetting is controlled by replay?

Hypothesis. The campaign's null on the cosine arm (WA-G: MEXA -0.0004, AaR@10 -0.009 against control, CVP 1.000, per the scorecard) was obtained at a single weight chosen to balance a different objective at initialization (0.02) and without replay (Yoruba perplexity 116 to 585 in the control itself), so its capability criterion could not discriminate. The proposal's hypothesis under test: with the degenerate minimum removed by construction and forgetting controlled, a word-level alignment term at the dip layer with a learned language-identity vector improves tail alignment on trained languages without cost to unseen languages, at some weight. The stated prior from the campaign is that it will not; the sweep is what makes the null informative. Two amendments from the judges are adopted: the CVP band is a monitored stop rule, not a by-construction guarantee, because the LM loss and replay move scale on their own; and because AaR@10 is a cosine reading of the same kind as the loss being optimized, a retrieval gain alone is a reading-only result and cannot pass the arm; behavior (context use, perplexity) and the anisotropy check carry the verdict.

Frozen predictions.
- P1 (scale band, monitored). CVP on the final checkpoint in [0.95, 1.05] and mean dip-layer norm within 5 percent of the control, every arm, every seed; a breach halts the arm at the monitoring step where it occurs and the arm is reported as infeasible at that step.
- P2 (the proposal's hypothesis). At the best weight, trained-language AaR@10 at layer 8 exceeds the control by at least 0.020 (two pooled across-seed standard deviations from WA-B 0.002 and WA-G 0.014) in 3 of 3 seeds. Below 0.020: not detectable.
- P3 (no trained-language special case). Held-out-language AaR@10 (fra, rus, arb, zho-CN, ben, yor, zul, ind) does not fall below the control by more than 0.020 in any arm that passes P2.
- P4 (replay makes the capability criterion discriminating). The control's geometric-mean held-out perplexity ratio against the frozen model is at most 1.45 (the code-switch control with 25 percent replay sat at 1.415); each alignment arm stays within the control plus one percentage point; yor and zul, outside the replay set, are reported separately and the highest-weight arm is predicted to exceed the control by more than one point on them.
- P5 (exam guard). Belebele 41-task accuracy within 1.0 point of the control for every arm (a guard, not a discriminator; the 89-percent-collapsed campaign arm matched its control at 0.395 versus 0.390).
- P6 (context use, powered). Cross-lingual context gain at 200 pairs x 6 trained languages pooled (1,200 paired differences, standard error about 0.014 nats): minimum detectable difference 0.03 nats against the control; below that, not detectable.
- P7 (alignment specificity, the Goodhart check). Mean cosine between matched word pairs minus mean cosine between random unmatched pairs rises in every alignment arm relative to the control; if unmatched cosine rises by more than half the matched rise, the term is being satisfied by anisotropy rather than alignment and the arm fails regardless of P2. Effective rank must stay at or above 0.90 of the control.
- P8 (verdict rule). An arm passes only if P2 and P6 both clear their thresholds with P1, P3, P4, P5, P7 holding, in 3 of 3 seeds. P2 without P6 is "reading-only" and does not pass. If P2 fails at all three weights while P1 and P4 hold, 3.2.2 is recorded as sound but inert at this scale and closed. If an arm passes, stage 2 (2,000 steps) runs and a scale-up to one 7 to 9B model is proposed separately.

Models and data. Qwen3-0.6B-Base, dip layer 8. Training: the Aim 1 word alignments for deu, fra, ces, hin, swa, ben (two-aligner agreement 0.61 to 0.75; 8,334 aligned sentence pairs from NTREX sentences 500 to 1900) plus the 25 percent replay mixture from train_codeswitch.py load_replay. Evaluation: NTREX first 300 sentences on the 12-language trained/held-out split of evaluate.py; 200 document-adjacent pairs for context use; Belebele 41 tasks; per-language perplexity including yor and zul.

Method. Arms at 400 steps, 3 seeds, Adafactor, lr 1e-5, identical except the objective: R-B control (LM on both sides of the aligned pairs plus replay); R-G1, R-G2, R-G3 (R-B plus the cosine word-level term with learned language-identity vector at weights 0.02, 0.2, 2.0; the term is bounded in [0, 2], so 2.0 puts it at the LM loss's scale); R-G3-noLV (weight 2.0 without the language vector). Word representations by averaging subword pieces at layer 8. Every checkpoint: full 29-layer LFS profile with components, raw concept variance, CVP, norm, MEXA, AaR@{1,5,10,20} on trained and held-out groups, per-language perplexity, context use at 200 pairs, Belebele 41 tasks, matched-minus-unmatched cosine. Contrasts against R-B. Stage 2 (conditional): best weight and control at 2,000 steps, 3 seeds. A dated addendum to prereg/PREREG_WORDALIGN.md freezes P1 to P8 before launch.

Controls. R-B control arm with replay; no-language-vector variant; held-out language group; unmatched-pair cosine; yor and zul outside replay.

Monitoring readings. Every 20 steps: LM loss, cosine term, language-vector norm, mean |h| at layer 8, CVP against the frozen reference, batch LFS with its pure-noise value (0.300 at the training batch shape), effective rank, stop-rule flags. Per checkpoint as above.

Behavioral targets. Cross-lingual context gain at 200 pairs (co-primary with P2), held-out and unseen-language perplexity, Belebele 41-task accuracy.

Cost. Stage 1: 15 arm-seeds x 0.4 GPU-h training (campaign wall clock 1,170 s per 400 steps for word-level arms, plus replay) = 6; 16 checkpoints x about 0.55 evaluation (evaluate 0.08, context use 0.05, Belebele 0.4) = 9; total about 9 after overlap with existing frozen evaluations. Stage 2: 6 runs x 2.0 + 6 x 0.55 = about 15.

Code to write. Replay mixing inside train_wordalign.py (about 40 lines, ported from train_codeswitch.py), batch LFS and effective-rank logging on a probe grid inside the word-level loop (about 30 lines), stop-rule enforcement with a stop-step record (about 30 lines), the matched-minus-unmatched cosine reading in evaluate.py (about 40 lines), a criteria table for the scorecard (about 80 lines), the prereg addendum. Existing: train_wordalign.py already carries the cosine option (arm WA-G), the language vector, --word_weight, and rep_norm logging; evaluate.py, context_use.py --n_pairs, rmfs/scripts/sbatch/belebele_arms.sbatch, scorecard.py.

Risks. Cosine can be raised for all pairs at once by concentrating variance in a few directions (rogue dimensions), hence P7 and effective rank. Aligner noise bounds the achievable signal. Weight 2.0 may swamp the LM loss (P4 catches it). 400 steps may be too short (stage 2 partly addresses). The context-use criterion cannot detect effects of the size the campaign observed (about 0.005 nats), stated so that not-detectable is not read as failure to measure. One model, one layer, six languages.

## 4. Stop rules and what would change our mind

Stop rules (mechanical, applied before any interpretation).
- Any trained arm whose monitoring-grid CVP falls below 0.90, whose dip-layer norm ratio leaves [0.80, 1.25] (W3: [0.95, 1.05]), or whose effective rank falls below 0.90 of the control is halted and reported as infeasible at its stop step; the optimizer never sees these rules.
- Any hook condition whose raw concept variance ratio leaves [0.98, 1.02] is a bug, not a result; the run is repeated after the fix.
- Any reading that moves favorably while CVP or norm is outside its band is scored as a failure, whatever LFS or MEXA say.
- Any effect below its preregistered minimum detectable effect is reported as not detectable and returns no verdict.
- A random-direction control that moves a reading as much as the true offset voids that reading for that experiment.
- W1 extension does not launch until W0 P1 and P5 pass and the pilot's manipulation check passes. W4a does not launch unless W1 returns Reading A. W4c does not launch unless W0 P7 and W2 A2 both survive. W4b does not launch without a scope decision.

What would change our mind.
- If W0 P2 shows retrieval readings near-invariant to the additive offset, the budget's 65 to 98 percent is reported as a Euclidean statement with no retrieval consequence, and offset interventions are evaluated on behavior only.
- If W0 P6 finds centered-and-scaled MLP accuracy above 0.30 in several models, the additive decomposition is missing language information a discriminator can see; the reading family needs a nonlinear separability reading before any monitor role is trusted, and the adversarial arm of 3.2.3 is reopened as a diagnostic.
- If W1 returns Reading B in the pilot and in at least two of the three small models, the dip-layer offset is behaviorally inert at inference time; the offset-targeting idea leaves Aim 2, W4a is cancelled, and Aim 1 readings keep only the layer-selector and monitor roles.
- If W1 returns Reading C, the language-versus-concept split fails as a construct for behavior; this is reported as a construct failure, not softened, and the technical report's interpretation of the budget is revised.
- If W1 returns Reading A with the joint rule holding (transfer up, perplexity cost bounded, Belebele flat, control silent) in at least 5 of 8 models, that is the first co-movement of structure and behavior under an intervention with no optimizer, and the statement that the observational association does not survive intervention is narrowed to alignment-loss interventions.
- If W2 P1 fails for parallel text, no data composition moved anything at 400 or 2,000 steps at this budget, and the data sub-aim is treated as a pretraining-scale question beyond this project's compute. If CS-S passes where CS-PAR fails, sentence-level alternation is the one code-switching form carried forward.
- If W3 passes P2 but not P6 at every weight, that is a second instance of retrieval readings improving without behavior, and Aim 2 is redirected toward data methods and preference training over representation losses. If P1 fires without any alignment term at weight 0.02, scale is not preserved by the cosine form under joint training and the by-construction claim is withdrawn.
- If W0 P7 shows |rho| with exposure above 0.85 in most models, or W2 A2 fails, the per-language share is a descriptive statistic and never an allocation key.
- Independently of outcomes: any arm that moves a retrieval reading favorably while raw concept variance or norm falls outside its band is a failure. That rule is what the campaign paid for and it does not change.

## 6. What this is not

- No arm places LFS, any variance-ratio quantity, MEXA, AaR, or CVP in a loss. This includes the batch-standardized matched-pair squared-difference proposal from one panel program: standardized matched-pair MSE is, up to constants, one minus the concept share of a fixed total variance, which is the campaign's ratio objective under another name and re-enters the family the Pearson +0.924 reversal falsified. It is dropped.
- No RMFS or any composite scalar for arm selection or ranking.
- No unbounded alignment term without a scale constraint by construction. The proposal's raw word-level and segment-level squared-difference losses have a minimum at zero representation and reached it; they are not run again, with or without a bounded guard, because a bounded penalty cannot restrain an unbounded term at any weight. The only alignment term in this protocol is the cosine form, whose scale invariance removes that minimum, and its scale band is still monitored as a stop rule because joint training with the LM loss can move scale on its own.
- No adversarial discriminator as a training loss. Its accuracy is saturated at 0.875 to 0.886 across all 29 layers on Qwen3-0.6B (chance 0.042), so it carries no layer-selective signal, and the quantity it removes is the one whose reduction reversed under optimization. W0 delivers its diagnostic content on CPU.
- No word- or phrase-level code-switching as an improvement method; W2 closes the sub-aim with the two missing comparisons and then stops.
- No weight sweeps, layer searches, or magnitude tuning against the readings; every layer is read from the LFS profile, every magnitude is 1.0 in confirmatory runs, and the one weight sweep (W3) is preregistered at three fixed values with a verdict rule that requires behavior.
- No new frozen-model measurement; the 17-model grid, the 14 dumps, the 19-model content-transfer analysis, the Belebele-0 runs, the budget, whitened, tailcurve, and dip-bootstrap artifacts are reused as they stand.
- LFS remains a measurement. In every experiment it selects the layer, checks that the manipulation did what it claims (with its raw components, since the ratio alone is blind to collapse), and monitors for collapse alongside CVP, norm, and effective rank. MEXA is computed alongside as a published reference measure. Neither is a success criterion.
